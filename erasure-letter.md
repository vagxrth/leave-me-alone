# Data erasure request template

Blocking stops new collection. It does not delete what is already held — that takes a written request.

Under India's Digital Personal Data Protection framework you are a **data principal**, and you may ask for confirmation of what is held about you, withdrawal of consent, and erasure. The substantive obligations are being phased in and most take effect on **13 May 2027**, so a request sent today may be answered with a form reply. Send it anyway: a dated, specific request that goes unanswered is exactly the record you will want later.

## Before you write

Run the [scanner](https://vagxrth.github.io/leave-me-alone/) on any storefront and note your `networkId`. A request that quotes the identifier is materially harder to deflect than one that does not, because it can be looked up rather than merely searched for.

If you have received a WhatsApp message from a shop you never gave your number to, **screenshot it**. A dated Business-API message that names a product you only looked at is the strongest single attachment you can send.

## Who to send it to

Send it **twice** — once to the platform and once to each brand. They have different roles and different obligations, and the brand is the party you actually have a relationship with.

| | Where |
|---|---|
| The platform | `Grievance-officer@gokwik.co`, cc `merchantcare@gokwik.co` |
| Each brand | The grievance officer or privacy contact in that brand's own privacy policy |

## Template

> **Subject:** Data erasure and consent withdrawal request — visitor identity record
>
> To the Grievance Officer,
>
> I am a data principal whose personal data is processed through the KwikPass visitor-identification module deployed on merchant websites. I have not knowingly created an account with you, and I have not given consent for my phone number to be linked to my browsing activity across unrelated merchants.
>
> **Identifiers**
> Network identifier (`kp_user_id`): `PASTE_YOUR_ID_HERE`
> Phone number: `+91 XXXXXXXXXX`
>
> I request the following:
>
> 1. **Confirmation** of all personal data held against these identifiers, including phone number, email address, device or visitor identifiers, delivery addresses, order history, and the cross-merchant visit record stored on my device under the key `usr_trck`.
> 2. **Disclosure** of which merchants have been given access to that record, and of any third parties — including messaging and marketing providers — to whom it has been disclosed.
> 3. **The lawful basis** relied on to associate my phone number with my device on websites where I have never entered it, and the date and mechanism by which any consent is said to have been obtained.
> 4. **Erasure** of all of the above, including derived identifiers, device fingerprints, and copies held by processors.
> 5. **Withdrawal** of any consent recorded against me, including any shared or network-wide marketing consent applied across merchants.
> 6. **Confirmation in writing** once the erasure is complete.
>
> Please respond within 30 days.
>
> Regards,
> `YOUR NAME`
> `DATE`

## For a brand, add this paragraph

> On `DATE`, at `TIME`, I visited your website. I did not log in, create an account, add anything to a cart, or enter my phone number at any point. On the same day I received a WhatsApp message from your business at `NUMBER`, sent as a Business API template. A screenshot is attached.
>
> Please tell me specifically: where you obtained my phone number, what record of my browsing you held at the time of sending, and what documented opt-in you rely on for that message under WhatsApp's Business Messaging Policy.

That last question is the one that tends to produce a real answer rather than a form reply, because a brand cannot answer it by pointing at its privacy policy.

## Separately: report the message to Meta

This is independent of Indian law and often faster. In WhatsApp, open the chat, tap the business name, and choose **Report**. The report reaches Meta carrying the template that was sent and the account that sent it — which is the enforcement path with actual consequences: template rejection, quality-rating downgrade, and at volume, messaging limits.

Report in particular where:

- a marketing message arrived with **no opt-out** of any kind, or
- the chat header claims you are receiving *"offers and announcements"* from a business you never opted in to.

Both are breaches of Meta's own policy on their face.

## Keep the replies

Whatever comes back — including nothing at all — keep it with the date you sent the request. From **13 May 2027**, when the DPDP Act's substantive obligations take effect, that correspondence becomes the evidence for a complaint to the Data Protection Board of India.
